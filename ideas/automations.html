Why it's worth hundreds a monthYour minimum-wage logic — and when it's true.

An office employee costs a Greek business roughly 1,200–1,500€ a month all in. If a system takes over 10 hours a week of copying, chasing and reminding, that's a quarter of a person. Charging 300–500€ a month for it is cheap — if four things are true:

  • They have people doing that work. Offices of 3–15 people, not a solo professional with no staff (those get the small tier).
  • It really removes hours — and we measure them before and after, with their numbers.
  • It runs without them watching it. Reminders go out, reports arrive, files sort themselves.
  • They clearly win. Our rule: they save at least 3× what they pay. A full salary is where you end up after it's proven, not where you start.

Finding their problemsThe 45-minute visit — «Ο χάρτης της εβδομάδας σας».

You don't need to have worked in their industry. You need to ask the right questions and listen. Bring a notebook, like the page you drew today.

  1. The week, out loud 15 min«Περιγράψτε μου μια συνηθισμένη εβδομάδα — από τη Δευτέρα το πρωί.» Write down every job that repeats. Ask the owner AND whoever sits at the front desk — the second person knows where the hours go.
  2. Four questions per job 15 minHow often? How long each time? Who does it? What goes wrong when it's forgotten?
  3. Draw the dream screen 10 min«Αν άνοιγες το πρωί μια σελίδα και έβλεπες ό,τι χρειάζεσαι — τι θα έγραφε;» Let them draw it on paper. That drawing is your spec.
  4. Collect samples 5 minPhotos of their Excel, a paper form, a typical Viber message, the reminder they send by hand. Never passwords.
  5. Pick ONE jobScore every job: hours per week × people doing it × how much it annoys them (1–3). The highest score is the free pilot. Everything else waits.

What we buildThe same blocks, arranged their way.

The organiser

One clean page in their colours — the «dream screen» they drew. Cards for their clients / patients / properties / cases, search, filters, stages. Works on the phone.

The reminders

Rules like «30 days before a renewal», «6 months after a cleaning», «3 days without the documents». Each sends a ready message (e-mail now; SMS / Viber where they want) and a note to the right staff member.

The morning report

On their phone at 8:00: what came in, what's missing, what's due today, the 3 numbers that matter.

The client links

Each of their customers gets a link to send documents or see a status — like your Chats.

The reader

AI that reads invoices, forms or statements and fills the cards — a person always checks and approves.

Backups & export

Automatic copies in two places, an export button, a monthly restore test. Their data, always theirs.

Where the data livesSafe, in Europe, always theirs.

Small

1–3 people · salon, solo practice

The same setup as your CRM: one page, data encrypted with their passphrase, stored privately, hourly snapshots, a weekly e-mailed safety copy, an export button. Cost to us ≈ 0–10€ a month.

Office

3–15 people with their own logins

A real database in the EU (e.g. Supabase, Frankfurt region): a login per person, permissions (the secretary doesn't see the money), daily backups, a log of who changed what. ≈ 25$ a month per client.

With an ERP

Companies like Nef Nef

We read from their program (SoftOne, Entersoft, Pylon, Epsilon…) — we never change it. Reports and reminders live in our layer next to it.

Rules on every project

  • Their data stays theirs — export any time, everything handed over if they leave.
  • EU servers only, a signed data agreement (DPA), the minimum personal data needed.
  • Two backups in two places, and once a month we actually restore one to prove it works.
  • Read-only first. Nothing is ever submitted to the tax office or changed in their program automatically — a person approves.
  • Monitoring: if a reminder fails to send or a backup is late, we know before they do — like your portfolio watchdog.

Your worries → answersEverything you told me today.

I don't know what other businesses do or what they repeat.
Nobody does before the visit — consultants included. The 45-minute method finds it, the niche tables tell you what to expect, and the client draws the answer for you.
They already have an ERP or a CRM.
Most do — and they still copy, chase and remind by hand, because their program is generic and complicated. We don't replace it; we sit next to it and do the boring jobs around it.
Custom means endless work.
It's custom arrangement of the same blocks, not custom code from zero. The scope of each phase is written down, and changes after that come from the retainer hours.
What if we lose their data?
Two backups in two places, restored once a month as a test, EU servers, an export button. It's the same safety net that already protects your own CRM — plus a real database for offices.
What if something breaks while I'm busy?
Monitoring warns us before the client notices, and nothing critical depends on a person pressing a button. The worst case is a reminder sent a few hours late — never lost data.
Will they pay hundreds a month?
Only if they clearly win — that's why we measure hours in the free pilot and keep the price at a third of what they save.
How will they change things without Claude?
They ask on their chat page; Claude does it; you approve. The retainer includes the hours.
